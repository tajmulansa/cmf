# The Last Emperor's Crown — Treatment

Style: **Gilded Illumination** (Lemo-Opuscar). English voice and subtitles. 1920×1080, 30 fps, about 78 s.
Topic: the British East India Company against the Mughal Empire, 1857: the uprising, the siege of Delhi, the fall of the last Mughal emperor, and the end of Company rule.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The crown's journey (chosen).** One object, the Mughal crown, lit and unlit. It opens dark on a bare gold ground (power gone, title kept), lights up once when the rebels proclaim Zafar emperor, sinks back through the siege, and ends as a single object on the end plaque while one last band of light rakes across it. A single object carries a big history in 78 s; the gold-lights-only-when-it-matters rule is the story itself.
2. **Two ledgers.** Split the picture between a Company ledger (brown ink, coins) and a Mughal court (gold, verses), a before/after that tips side to side. Clear, but the style wants one painted world, not a split screen, and it turns the film into a diagram.
3. **A countdown of days**: 10 May, 11 May, 14 September, 21 September… stations on a road from Meerut to Rangoon. Strong for dates, weak for feeling; the emperor would be a passenger in his own story.

Chosen: **1**, because the style's signature (gold that only shines when a light crosses it) is exactly the arc of 1857 for the Mughals: a title with no power, one bright summer, then dark. The Company's fall is the turn in the last beat: the picture shows both empires ending.

Opening image: a gold ground with nothing on it, then the first stroke of colour (the red walls of the fort brushed in). Ending: a locked frame on the crown lying on the end plaque, a band of light raking across it once; the plaque is lettered "1526 – 1857".

## 2. Logline and arc

An old poet who is emperor only in name is handed his empire back by rebel soldiers for one summer, and loses it, and the trading company that took it from him falls with him.

- **Setup** (0–20 s): the emperor of a fort; a company that runs India.
- **Turn** (20–40 s): Meerut, the spark; the ride to Delhi; the crown lights up.
- **Fall** (40–64 s): siege, breach, surrender, his sons killed. Serious register, no jokes.
- **Ending** (64–78 s): exile; the Company abolished; the crown alone on the plaque.

## 3. Benchmark

- **The Secret of Kells** (animated feature, 2009) for flat-pigment animation that still moves the camera with purpose. Learn: depth by overlap and scale only, camera that travels across one painted plane, ornament as frame. Don't take: its characters, knotwork designs, colour script or music.
- **Mughal court miniatures (17th century, public domain as a genre)** for composition: tilted architecture seen from above and front at once, a ruler framed by an arch, crowds in rows, continuous narration. Don't take: any specific painting's layout or figures; never name a manuscript or painter in the film.

## 6. Cue map

- Tempo 72 bpm (beat 0.833 s), 4/4, bar 3.33 s. Mode: D dorian; the touch lifts to D major (Picardy third); the fall returns to D aeolian; the end resolves on a bare fifth, not major.
- Band (style palette): bowed solo fiddle, harp, soft organ, frame drum, recorder; tubular bells and glockenspiel for gold.
  - 0–20 s: organ drone D + harp arpeggios, sparse; glock on the first gold stroke.
  - 20–34 s: frame drum enters (heartbeat, quarters), fiddle line low; accelerate to eighths on the ride.
  - 34.5–35.4: **silence** (drone only, then cut), bell on the touch at 35.4; full bar: fiddle + harp + organ, D major.
  - 41.5–54: drum in halves, organ minor, fiddle long notes; cannon thumps on the downbeats of bars.
  - 54.5–59: **near-silence**: one held organ note, a far bell.
  - 59–67: harp alone, ship rhythm in 6/8 feel.
  - 67–78: recorder melody once, ending on an open fifth; one tubular bell when the light crosses the crown.
- Cuts land on bar lines; the touch, the breach and the plaque light land on downbeats.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Speech scroll: a vellum banderole fixed at bottom centre, one width for the whole film (set by the longest line), English only, IM Fell English 34 px, brown ink, pen-wipe reveal. Long lines split into two scroll cards.
- Title "THE LAST EMPEROR" in Uncial Antiqua 64 px on a scroll in shot 1; dates on the end plaque in IM Fell English with lining figures.
- Hero kept out of the lower-centre band while lines are up.
- No maps in the film (no borders to draw).

## 9. Facts (sources in FACTS.md)

Meerut rising 10 May 1857; rebels at Delhi 11 May, Zafar's support 12 May; British assault 14 Sep, city taken about 20 Sep; surrender at Humayun's tomb 21 Sep; his sons Mirza Mughal and Mirza Khizr Sultan and grandson Mirza Abu Bakr shot by Hodson 22 Sep; trial 1858, exile to Rangoon; Government of India Act 2 Aug 1858; Zafar born 1775 (81 in May 1857), died 7 Nov 1862 in Rangoon. Mughal rule from 1526 (Panipat).
